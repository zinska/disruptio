What we're building

A physical and cultural space in the heart of St. Louis where builders come together. Disruptio is a student-run initiative that helps WashU talent and the broader St. Louis community build ventures locally:

  • Mentorship from founders who have built and scaled companies
  • Resources and support to go from idea to traction
  • Social events and community that make building fun
  • Space for organic collaboration: nothing prescriptive, just serendipity
  • A bridge to coastal capital, connecting St. Louis ventures with investors in SF, New York, and Boston

Who it's for

You're already building

You have a side project, a startup, or a research idea you want to turn into a company, and you want people around you who take it as seriously as you do.

You want to start

You haven't built anything yet, but you want to. You'll find teammates, ideas, and people a few steps ahead who will help you get going.

Why St. Louis needs this

In 1904, St. Louis was booming. The World's Fair brought global attention. Washington University built Francis Field and the Brookings Quadrangle to host the Olympics. The city was at the center of the old economy, thriving with industry, culture, and ambition.

Today looks different. While Washington University has grown from a regional "streetcar college" into a world-leading research institution, the city hasn't shared in that success. There's no startup culture, no innovation ecosystem, and no gravitational pull keeping talent here.

"How do I get out of St. Louis and into San Francisco, New York, or Boston?"
— Every ambitious WashU senior

The brain drain is real. Sam Altman and Jack Dorsey both grew up in St. Louis and left to build elsewhere. The people who stayed, like Square co-founder Jim McKelvey, who built LaunchCode here, show what's possible when builders invest in the city. We want more of the second story.

Our approach

We can't replicate Silicon Valley overnight, but we can start the flywheel. The ecosystem starts as a bridge between St. Louis and coastal risk capital. Over time, ventures that start and scale here will create capital and density in St. Louis itself.

Fun is crucial

In San Francisco, New York, and Boston, building startups is fun, social, and cool. At WashU, it's often academic. We need a space where building is joyful, not just rigorous.

In St. Louis, for St. Louis

Here, the moment anyone gets traction, the question is "How do I get to the coast?" We want an ecosystem that makes staying the obvious choice: lower costs, strong universities, and growing density.
